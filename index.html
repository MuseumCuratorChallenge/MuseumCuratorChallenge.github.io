About the Challenge

Curate it. Analyze it.
Defend it, live.

The Archaeology Survey Association runs field programmes, research circles, and public-history events for students and early-career researchers across archaeology, museology, and heritage studies. The Museum Curator Challenge is our flagship virtual event: a chance to think like a curator rather than just study one.

Each team adopts one museum — grand or small, national or niche, physical or entirely digital — and builds the case for why it matters. That means tracing what the museum has actually contributed to fields like art history, science education, conservation, and cultural diplomacy, and then examining how it has changed hands with technology: from index-card catalogues to 3D-scanned collections, augmented-reality galleries, and AI-assisted archiving.

Research

Dig into archives, catalogues, and institutional history.

Analysis

Weigh the museum's real contribution across fields.

Design

Distill it all into ten slides that hold their nerve.

Presentation

Pitch it live, on camera, to a panel of judges.

Who It's For

Built for curious researchers

  • Undergraduate and postgraduate students in archaeology, history, museology, art, or heritage-adjacent fields
  • Pairs who want to research together, not solo — every team is exactly two people
  • Anyone comfortable presenting live on video, from anywhere in the world
  • Note: students in their final year of a degree are not eligible this cycle

The Brief

Four things your deck needs to prove

Your ten slides should build one clear argument for your chosen museum, covering these four pillars in whatever order suits your story.

Pillar A

Origins & Identity

Why was this museum founded, by whom, and what problem or gap was it built to address?

Pillar B

Field Contributions

What has it actually given back — to art, science, education, conservation, or public memory?

Pillar C

Technological Evolution

How did it move from physical ledgers to digitisation, 3D scanning, AR/VR, and AI cataloguing?

Pillar D

The Next Chapter

If you advised the museum's director tomorrow, what's the one technology or shift you'd push for?

Format: a maximum of 10 slides, submitted ahead of the event and presented live on Zoom by both teammates. Slides beyond the 10th will simply not be reviewed by the jury.

Rules & Format

Field protocol

Straightforward rules, strictly enforced, so every team is judged on the same ground.

Team & Conduct

Exactly two people per team — no solo entries, no groups of three or more.

Both teammates must share one video frame for the entire live presentation.

Final-year students are not eligible to register for this cycle of the challenge.

Cheating or misrepresentation of research is prohibited and results in immediate disqualification.

The jury's decisions on scoring and eligibility are final.

Format & Judging

Maximum 10 slides, submitted before the deadline and presented as-is on Zoom.

Each team gets roughly 8 minutes to present, followed by 4 minutes of jury questions.

FAQ

Questions from the field

Can I take part on my own, without a teammate?

No. Every team must have exactly two members — not one, and not three or more. If you don't have a partner yet, our FAQ community thread and campus ambassadors can help pair you with another registrant.

Are final-year students eligible?

Not for this cycle. Students in the final year of any degree programme are not eligible to register, for either teammate. All other years of study are welcome to apply.

Is there a registration fee?

No — entry is completely free. We only ask that registered teams actually show up and present, since spots are limited for the live Zoom finale.

Which museum can we choose?

Any museum, anywhere in the world — a national institution, a small regional collection, a university museum, or an entirely digital/virtual museum. Choose one you can genuinely research in depth.

What happens if we exceed 10 slides?

The jury will only review the first 10 slides of your deck. Anything beyond that simply won't be seen, so plan your ten slides carefully.

How strictly is the "single video frame" rule enforced?

Strictly. Both teammates need to be visible together in one camera frame for the full duration of your presentation and Q&A — this is how we keep the format fair for every team.

Do all participants receive a certificate?

Yes. Every registered team member who presents at the live event receives an e-certificate of participation, regardless of placement.
